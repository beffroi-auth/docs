# Pending requests

Some applications cannot open the sign-in pages in front of you: a television or another device without a
keyboard, or an application that asks for your approval from elsewhere. They ask the sign-in service instead, and
you answer from a browser where you are signed in. Two pages take those answers: the page where you type the code
a device shows, and **Pending requests**.

Allowing either kind records your agreement, and the application is then listed on [Allowed
applications](applications.md), where you can take it back.

## Connecting a device

A device that signs you in this way shows a code and the address of a page. Open that address in a browser, on your
phone or your computer, and sign in if that browser is not signed in yet.

1. The page **Connect a device** says **Enter the code your device is showing.** Type it under **Code**, with or
   without its hyphen, in capitals or not, and press **Continue**. When the device shows a link or a QR code that
   already carries the code, this step is skipped.
2. The page asks **Allow** the name of the application **to connect?** It says who is asking, the account you are
   signed in with, the code, where the request came from, and the list of what the application asks for.
3. Check that the code on the page is the one your device shows, then press **Allow** or **Refuse**.

**If you did not start this request, refuse it.** Somebody may be trying to have you approve their device.

| The page says | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Device connected** | The application can use your account. Go back to your device |
| **Request refused** | The application has no access to your account. You can close the page |
| **Unknown code. Check the code your device is showing.** | The code is wrong, has expired or was answered already. Check it, or start again on the device to get a new one |
| **Too many attempts. Wait a minute, then try again.** | Too many wrong codes were typed within a minute |

## Requests waiting for you

An application can ask for your approval without opening a page in front of you. The request then waits on
**Pending requests**, the first card of your account, and the column beside every page counts the requests that
wait. Depending on what your organisation offers, a [notification](notifications.md) can tell you that one is
waiting: it names the application and says **Open your account to answer.** Nothing in a notification answers a
request; you answer on this page.

The page first says who is asking, your organisation. Then each request says **asks to use your account.** after the
name of the application, the message the application wrote after **says:** when it wrote one, where the request
came from, and the list of what it asks for. Press **Allow** or **Refuse** under it.

**If you did not start a request, refuse it.**

| The page says | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Request allowed** | The application can use your account. Go back to where you started |
| **Request refused** | The application has no access to your account. You can close the page |
| **That request no longer needs an answer.** | It was answered already, waited too long and expired, or you revoked the access of its application |
| **No pending request.** | Nothing waits for you |

When the application asked for a stronger sign-in than the one you made, **Allow** takes you to the sign-in page
first, which says **A stronger sign-in is required.** and what will do. Once you have signed in, you come back to
**Pending requests**: press **Allow** again.

The page of each application on [Allowed applications](applications.md) also lists what it is waiting for from you,
with **See pending requests**.
