# Your password

**Your password** says when your password was last changed, **Last changed on** followed by the date, and holds
the form that changes it. The page opens only when you signed in less than five minutes ago; otherwise the sign-in
page comes first.

## Changing it

Type your **Current password**, then the one you want under **New password** and again under **Confirm the new
password**, and press **Change password**.

As you type the new one, a word under the field says how hard it would be to guess, from **very weak** to **very
strong**. It is worked out in your browser and sends your password nowhere; the sign-in service decides when you
press the button.

**Changing your password signs you out of your other browsers.** The browser you changed it from stays signed in.
The change is [told to you](notifications.md) and listed in [Account activity](activity.md) as **Password changed**.

## What a password has to be

- **Long enough.** At least fifteen characters, or twelve where your organisation asks for a second step at every
  sign-in. The page names the length it asks for when a password is too short.
- **Not too long.** At most 4096 bytes, which is more than a thousand characters whatever the alphabet.
- **Not easy to guess.** A password made of a few characters used again and again is refused, unless it is long
  enough to make up for it.
- **Not in a data breach.** If your organisation checks passwords against lists of passwords published after data
  breaches, a password those lists hold is refused.

Nothing else is asked: no digit, capital or symbol is required.

## What is refused

| The page says | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Wrong current password.** | Type the password you use today. A mistake here does not count against your sign-in attempts |
| **Too many attempts. Wait a moment, then try again.** | A wrong current password was typed five times within a minute. Wait a minute, then try again |
| **That is already your password. Choose another one.** | Choose a password you do not use yet |
| **The two passwords do not match.** | Type the same new password in both fields |
| **The password must be at least 15 characters long.**, or 12 where every sign-in asks for a second step | Make it longer |
| **This password is too long.** | Make it shorter |
| **This password is too easy to guess. Make it longer or use several words.** | Make it longer, or use several words |
| **This password appears in a data breach. Choose another one.** | Choose another password, and use this one nowhere else either |
| **The password could not be checked. Try again.** | The list of breached passwords did not answer. Press the button again |

## A password found in a data breach

If your organisation checks passwords against those lists, it also checks the password you already use, from time
to time when you sign in with it. When a list holds it, **Your password** says **Your password appears in a data
breach**, then the date it was found in brackets and **Change it now.**, and the card of the page on your account
says **found in a breach**.

From then on, each time you sign in to your account, and on the way to some applications, the pages take you to
**Your password** first, and add **Change your password to continue. Otherwise you will be asked again at your next
sign-in.** Once you change it, you go on to the page you were going to. Other applications let you through, and
**Your password** keeps saying it until you change it.

## No password

**You have no password: you sign in another way.** means your account holds no password. You cannot create a
password from this page; your organisation can send you
[a link](#a-new-password-from-a-link) to choose one, if it allows passwords.

If you sign in through your organisation's own sign-in service, the page says **You sign in through your
organisation: there is nothing to set up here.**

## A new password from a link

If you have forgotten your password, or never had one, your organisation can send you a link that sets one without
the old one and without signing in. It opens the page **Choose a new password**, which names your account and says
**This link works only once.** and **All your sessions will end: sign in again with the new password.**

Type the password twice, under **New password** and **Confirm the new password**, and press **Save the password**.
The rules and the messages are the ones above; **Your organisation does not allow a password for your account.**
means your organisation signs you in another way. Once the password is saved, every session of your account ends,
and the page takes you to your account, where you sign in with it.

The link works once, within 72 hours, and a new one makes the previous one stop working. A link that no longer works
opens a page that says **This link was already used or has expired. Ask for a new one.**
