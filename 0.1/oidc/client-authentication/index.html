    Client authentication¶

    A client authenticates with the one method it registered and with no other (RFC 6749 §2.3.1). The method is read wherever the provider has to know which client is calling: the token endpoint, the introspection and revocation endpoints, the pushed authorization request endpoint, the device authorization endpoint and the backchannel authentication endpoint. A request that presents another method than the registered one is refused exactly as a request that presents a wrong secret is.

    The methods¶

    The bundle wires seven, in this order:

    Method What the request carries What is checked What the client registers
    client_secret_basic Authorization: Basic, the identifier and the secret form-urlencoded and joined by a colon (RFC 6749 §2.3.1, RFC 7617 §2) The secret, against the hash the provider keeps A secret
    client_secret_post client_id and client_secret in the body (RFC 6749 §2.3.1) The same A secret
    private_key_jwt client_assertion, a JWT it signed with a key of its own (RFC 7523 §2.2, OIDC Core §9) The signature against the keys the client publishes, then the claims jwks or jwks_uri
    client_secret_jwt client_assertion, a JWT signed with its secret under HS256 (OIDC Core §9) The MAC with the secret the provider keeps, then the claims A secret
    tls_client_auth client_id, and a certificate at the TLS handshake (RFC 8705 §2.1) The one value of RFC 8705 §2.1.2 it registered, read out of the certificate One of the five tls_client_auth_* values
    self_signed_tls_client_auth client_id, and a certificate at the TLS handshake (RFC 8705 §2.2) That the certificate is one the client publishes jwks or jwks_uri
    none client_id alone (RFC 6749 §2.1) Nothing: the client names itself Nothing

    A client that registers no method takes client_secret_basic, the first one wired for a declared client and the default of RFC 7591 §2 for one that registers itself. What the wiring holds is what a client may register and what the discovery document announces, so a method taken out of the wiring is neither accepted nor announced.

    Member of the discovery document What it lists
    token_endpoint_auth_methods_supported, revocation_endpoint_auth_methods_supported The seven methods, in the order above
    introspection_endpoint_auth_methods_supported The six that authenticate a client: none is refused there (RFC 7662 §2.1)
    token_endpoint_auth_signing_alg_values_supported and its two siblings What an assertion may be signed with: RS256, PS256, ES256 and EdDSA for private_key_jwt, HS256 for client_secret_jwt

    A shared secret¶

    client_secret_basic and client_secret_post present the secret itself, and the provider keeps only a hash of it, made by the password hasher of the core and compared by it. A request whose client is unknown, or registered for another method, pays the same verification against a decoy hash, so the time an answer takes says nothing about which clients exist.

    client_secret_basic reads a header made of Basic, whatever its case, then spaces or tabs, then one token (RFC 7235 §2.1). A token that does not decode to an identifier and a secret joined by a colon, an empty one included, is invalid_request; a header of another form is not one the method reads. client_secret_post takes the secret from a body that also names the client in client_id, and a client_secret without one is invalid_request.

    client_secret_jwt signs an assertion with the secret, so the provider has to hold the secret to compute the same MAC: it keeps the secret itself, sealed in the row of a client that registered itself. That is the cost of the method: a compromised process of the provider holds what computes the MAC as well.

    An assertion¶

    private_key_jwt and client_secret_jwt carry the same assertion, in the same two parameters:

    POST /token HTTP/1.1
Content-Type: application/x-www-form-urlencoded

grant_type=authorization_code&code=...&redirect_uri=...&code_verifier=...
&client_assertion_type=urn%3Aietf%3Aparams%3Aoauth%3Aclient-assertion-type%3Ajwt-bearer
&client_assertion=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IjEifQ...

    client_assertion_type is urn:ietf:params:oauth:client-assertion-type:jwt-bearer and nothing else, and the two parameters come together or not at all. Which of the two methods reads the assertion is decided by its alg: HS256 is client_secret_jwt, an algorithm the provider signs with is private_key_jwt. The signature is verified first, and nothing the assertion says is believed before it is. Then:

    Claim What it has to say
    iss, sub The client_id, both of them. A client_id in the body, when there is one, names the same client
    aud One value, and only one: the issuer of the tenant. A client registered for the backchannel grant may name the token endpoint or the backchannel authentication endpoint instead, on either host the provider serves them on (CIBA Core §7.1)
    exp Present, not past, and no further ahead than the lifetime the provider accepts
    iat Present, and not in the future
    jti Present, and never seen before for that client: it is remembered until the exp in the beffroi.client_assertions cache pool, which a deployment of several machines points at a store they share

    The key is the client's and nobody else's. A kid in the header names one of the keys the client publishes, a header without one is tried against all of them, and a key that names another alg, or a use other than sig, is not tried. jwk, jku and x5c in the header are ignored (RFC 8725 §3.10).

    Keys behind a jwks_uri are read and kept. The URL is read with one GET, no redirect followed, any status but 2xx refused, within a time and a size of its own. The set is kept in the beffroi.client_keys cache pool, filed under its URL, and read again when an assertion names a kid the kept set does not hold, at most once per cooldown: a client that rotates its key is followed at once, and one that signs with a key it never published cannot make the provider call it on every request. A set that cannot be read refuses the authentication, and the reason goes to the audit trail.

    config/packages/beffroi.yaml
    beffroi:
    client_authentication:
        assertion_max_lifetime: 300
        assertion_leeway: 30
        jwks_timeout: 3.0
        jwks_max_size: 65536
        jwks_ttl: 86400
        jwks_refetch_cooldown: 300
    Option Default What it decides
    assertion_max_lifetime 300 Seconds ahead of the request the exp of an assertion may be (RFC 7523 §3)
    assertion_leeway 30 Seconds allowed of the clock of a client, both ways
    jwks_timeout 3.0 Seconds one read of a jwks_uri is given, connection included
    jwks_max_size 65536 Bytes a jwks_uri may answer before the read stops
    jwks_ttl 86400 Seconds a set read from a jwks_uri is kept
    jwks_refetch_cooldown 300 Seconds between two reads of one jwks_uri when an assertion names an unknown kid

    The same six options bound the assertions of the issuers a tenant trusts for the JWT bearer grant, and the reads of the sets they publish.

    A certificate¶

    tls_client_auth and self_signed_tls_client_auth read the same thing, the certificate of the connection, and the client names itself in client_id. The certificate is the one the TLS listener in front of the provider writes in the SSL_CLIENT_CERT server variable, never a header: a request that carries SSL-Client-Cert, X-SSL-Client-Cert, X-Client-Cert or X-Forwarded-Client-Cert is refused with invalid_request. Only the leaf reaches the provider, so the chain, the validity period and the revocation of the certificate are judged by the listener, which is where a deployment that turns tls_client_auth on says which authorities it accepts.

    • tls_client_auth compares one value read out of the certificate with the one the client registered: its subject distinguished name, written as RFC 4514 writes one and normalised when the client is read, so that openssl x509 -noout -subject -nameopt RFC2253 prints a name it can register as it is; or one entry of its subject alternative name, a DNS name compared without regard to case, a URI compared as text, an IP address compared as the octets it stands for, an email address compared as written.
    • self_signed_tls_client_auth authenticates a certificate the client publishes in its JWK Set, as the first entry of the x5c of a key (RFC 7517 §4.7) or by its x5t#S256 (§4.9). The certificate is compared, not the key inside it, so a certificate reissued on the same key is published before it is presented.

    Both bind the tokens of the request to the certificate that authenticated it (RFC 8705 §3.1). A certificate presented beside a secret or an assertion authenticates nothing: the listener asked for it, the request did not choose it. Whether it binds the tokens of such a client is what the client registered in tls_client_certificate_bound_access_tokens. Mutual TLS says how a deployment serves the host that asks for a certificate, and what a bound token is then.

    A public client¶

    none is not an authentication, and it is announced as what it is: the client names itself in client_id and proves nothing, which is what a native or a browser application can do. The introspection endpoint refuses it with invalid_client, describing a token only to a client that proves who it is (RFC 7662 §2.1), and a client that registers it is refused client_credentials and requires_pkce: false. What protects a public client is PKCE and its exact redirect URI, never this method.

    How a request is read, and refused¶

    The request presents one mechanism or it is refused before anything is verified: credentials in the Authorization header and others in the body are two mechanisms (RFC 6749 §2.3), and so are a secret and an assertion. The two certificate methods read one thing and are one mechanism, the registration of the client saying which of them verifies it. An Authorization header no method reads, of another scheme than Basic or not of its form, is refused when nothing else in the request proves the client, a public client naming itself included; beside a credential of the body or a certificate of the connection that proves the client, it is not read.

    What the request does Error Status
    A credential parameter that is not a single string, Basic credentials that do not decode, half of an assertion, another client_assertion_type, an assertion that is no JWT invalid_request 400
    Two mechanisms at once invalid_request 400
    A client_id in the body that names another client than the credentials invalid_request 400
    An Authorization header no method reads, and nothing else that proves the client, or no credential at all invalid_client 401
    An unknown client, a method other than the registered one, a wrong secret, an assertion refused or replayed, keys that cannot be read, a certificate that is not the client's invalid_client, described Client authentication failed. 401

    Every refusal of the last line reads the same, so an answer does not say whether a client exists or which method it registered; why it was refused goes to the audit trail, with the client and the method, as a ClientAuthenticationFailed event. A 401 carries WWW-Authenticate: Basic realm="<issuer>" when the client used the Authorization header, and no challenge otherwise (RFC 6749 §5.2).

    A client that authenticates counts one request against its own load budget, load.client, 1200 requests in 60 seconds by default, here and nowhere else, this being the one place its identifier is proven rather than claimed. A public client counts nothing there, the budget of the address it calls from bounding it. A spent budget is answered 429 with a Retry-After.
