    Logout¶

    Three specifications, and they are not interchangeable. RP-Initiated Logout 1.0 is a client asking for a session to end; Front-Channel Logout 1.0 tells the other clients of that session through the browser; and Back-Channel Logout 1.0 tells them server to server, with a signed logout token. A provider that implements only the first one has not logged anybody out of anywhere else. The provider implements the three, and announces them in its discovery document: end_session_endpoint, and frontchannel_logout_supported, frontchannel_logout_session_supported, backchannel_logout_supported and backchannel_logout_session_supported set to true.

    The end session endpoint¶

    GET or POST at /logout, a page of the interaction plane (RP-Initiated Logout 1.0 §2). The parameters are read from the query of a GET and from the form body of a POST.

    Parameter What the provider does with it
    id_token_hint An ID token the provider issued: signed by a key the tenant publishes, its iss the issuer, a sub, and a sid when it carries one. Its exp is not read, a hint describing a session that may be long past. It names the client, by the first of its audiences that is a client of the tenant
    client_id The client, when the request names it. With a hint, it must be one of the audiences of the hint
    post_logout_redirect_uri Where the browser goes once the logout is done: one of the post_logout_redirect_uris the client registered, compared byte for byte (§3). It needs a client, named by the hint or by client_id
    state Given back with the redirection, printable ASCII
    ui_locales The language of the pages the browser is shown
    logout_hint Read as text without control characters, and carried to the confirmation page. It decides nothing

    What is refused is a page, never a redirection (§4): a hint that is not a signed compact JWT, or whose audiences name no client of the tenant, a client_id the tenant does not know or that is not an audience of the hint, a post_logout_redirect_uri the client did not register or that no client is named for, a parameter of the wrong shape. The browser is shown the error page of the provider, status 400, with the error invalid_request and its description; nothing reaches the client.

    A hint the provider cannot verify is read as no hint. A hint signed by a key the tenant no longer publishes, or one that does not say what an ID token of the provider says, names no client: the person is asked, and a post_logout_redirect_uri that only that hint could have justified is dropped rather than refused (§6).

    What happens to the session¶

    The session is the one the browser holds with the tenant.

    • No active session: there is nothing to end. The browser is sent to the post_logout_redirect_uri with its state, or shown the page that says it is signed out.
    • A hint that proves the session: its sub, read for the client the hint was issued to, names the person signed in, and its sid is the session the browser holds. The session ends at once, without a question. A client of pairwise identifiers proves it with the identifier of its sector, as a client of public identifiers does with the subject the account carries.
    • Anything else: no hint, a hint that could not be verified, a hint of another person or of nobody, a hint of another session or with no sid. The person is asked, on a page that names the client when one is named and the person signed in, with two buttons, Sign out and Stay signed in. The page posts the same parameters back with the answer and a form token; a form posted without a valid token is refused with a page, status 403. Stay signed in ends nothing and sends the browser nowhere: the page says the person is still signed in and that nothing was changed.

    Once the session has ended, the browser goes, in this order, through the front-channel logout page when a client of the session registered a frontchannel_logout_uri, then through the SAML service providers that signed in under the session, as single logout describes, and lands at the post_logout_redirect_uri with its state and no iss, which RFC 9207 gives authorization responses only, or on the page that says the browser is signed out. When no client of the session registered a front-channel URI and no service provider signed in under it, the browser goes straight there, with a 302 to the post_logout_redirect_uri when there is one.

    The confirmation page names, in its form-action, the origin of the post_logout_redirect_uri and of the single logout service of each SAML service provider the chain would go to: a browser checks that directive over every redirection of a submission.

    A request posted from another site arrives without the session cookie, which is SameSite=Lax, and would find no session to end. The provider posts it again from its own origin first, so that the session it ends is the one the browser holds and the confirmation page is one the browser can answer. It reads where a post comes from in Sec-Fetch-Site, and serves a browser that does not send it as the request came.

    Front-channel logout¶

    Once a browser has ended its session, it is given a page that loads one frame per client of that session that registered a frontchannel_logout_uri (Front-Channel Logout 1.0 §2, §3).

    The clients of a session are the clients that exchanged an authorization code issued in it, each once, in the order of the first code each was issued; a client no longer registered is skipped. The back channel tells the same clients.

    The URI of each frame is the one the client registered, with iss and sid added as query parameters when the client registered frontchannel_logout_session_required: true, and as it is otherwise, §2 making the two conditional.

    The page loads nothing but those frames. Its Content-Security-Policy is default-src 'none', with the origins of the frames in frame-src, the one script of the page named by its hash in script-src, and form-action 'none', base-uri 'none' and frame-ancestors 'none'. It is served with Cache-Control: no-store. The script moves the browser on once every frame has loaded or failed, and after three seconds at the latest, since a logout has to end even when a client never answers; a browser that runs no script is moved on after the same three seconds. With nowhere to go, the page has no script and says the browser may be closed.

    What it can promise is what it says. A frame of another origin reaches its client only if the browser sends that client its cookies, and a browser that blocks third-party cookies does not. The page says the provider asked the applications, those the browser let it reach were told, and the others sign the person out when they are next used: it does not say the person is signed out everywhere. The back channel is what does not depend on a browser.

    Back-channel logout¶

    Every session that ends is announced to each client of that session that registered a backchannel_logout_uri, with one logout token each (Back-Channel Logout 1.0 §2.6), whatever ended it: the announcement is made on the end of the session, never on the request that asked for it.

    The logout token¶

    A JWT signed by the active key of the tenant, under the algorithm the client registered for its ID tokens, id_token_signed_response_alg, or the algorithm the provider signs with by default when it registered none, since that is the one the client knows how to verify (§2.6). Its header names typ: logout+jwt, alg and kid.

    Claim Value
    iss The issuer
    sub The subject identifier the client knows the person by, public or pairwise, never the identifier of the account
    aud The client
    iat When the token was minted
    exp iat plus the budget of its delivery below, two hundred and thirty-five seconds by default
    jti A UUID drawn for this token
    events {"http://schemas.openid.net/event/backchannel-logout": {}}
    sid The session that ended, always, whether the client registered backchannel_logout_session_required or not

    It carries no nonce, which is how a client tells it from an ID token (§2.4). The key that signed it stays in the JWK Set at least until that exp, so a client that receives the token late but in time can still verify it (discovery and JWKS).

    Its delivery¶

    The token is minted on the lane of the events once the session has ended, and carried to the client on the lane of the notifications: a client that cannot be reached holds no logout back, and nothing a client does reaches the person, whose session is already closed.

    One request per attempt: a POST of application/x-www-form-urlencoded whose only parameter is logout_token (§2.5), with no cookie and no authentication, read for its status code and nothing else. No redirect is followed: the URI a client registered is where the token goes, and nobody else moves it.

    The client answers What the provider does
    A 2xx status Done
    400 or 501, which §2.8 makes final Gives up at once
    A redirection Gives up at once: the same answer would come back every time, and the provider follows none. The client registers the URL it redirects to instead
    Any other status, or nothing within the timeout Sends it again later, waiting longer each time and never more than retry_max_delay seconds

    A client that refuses the token is given up on, and that is said, never kept quiet: an act of the journal, logout_token.given_up, naming the client and the person, so that somebody can see that a client was left believing its user signed in.

    config/packages/beffroi.yaml
    beffroi:
    back_channel_logout:
        timeout: 5.0
        retries: 6
        retry_delay: 2
        retry_multiplier: 3
        retry_max_delay: 60
    Option Default What it decides
    timeout 5.0 Seconds one attempt is given, connection included, 0.1 at least. A client that hangs holds no worker
    retries 6 How many retries the budget of a delivery counts
    retry_delay 2 Seconds before the first retry
    retry_multiplier 3 What each wait is multiplied by to give the next one
    retry_max_delay 60 Seconds a wait never exceeds

    These numbers make the budget a logout token is written for. With the defaults, the waits before the first six retries are 2, 6, 18, 54, 60 and 60 seconds, and two hundred seconds of waiting plus five seconds for each of the seven attempts they count make two hundred and thirty-five seconds. That budget is the exp of the token, and the key that signed it stays in the JWK Set at least that long once it retires: changing one of these numbers moves both.

    The budget bounds the token, not the sending. A client still out of reach when the budget has run out may be sent the token again, retry_max_delay seconds apart, and what it then receives is past its exp, which a client refuses as it would an expired ID token (§2.6). A client that answers within the budget receives a token it can act on; after it, it can only receive an expired one.

    What ends a session¶

    A session ends:

    • at the end session endpoint, as above;
    • on the pages of the account, when its person ends it, ends every other session, or changes their password, which ends every other session;
    • with beffroi:user:revoke-session, at the console;
    • by a LogoutRequest of a SAML service provider, at the single logout service;
    • when another person signs in on the browser that held it.

    Each end is an act, session.ended, and each one is announced on the back channel. Only the ones a browser ends itself, at the end session endpoint, on the page of the account for the session of that browser, and at the single logout service, go through the front-channel page and the SAML service providers.

    A session that reaches its expiry is not ended. It is no longer active, so an authorization code issued under it and not exchanged yet is refused with invalid_grant. But nothing is announced to its clients, and the access tokens and the refresh tokens issued under it keep their own lifetimes.

    Every end of a session revokes what was issued under it¶

    However a session ends, the refresh tokens issued under it are revoked, rotated ones included, and its opaque access tokens removed, on the lane of the events and dated by the end. The provider does not wait for that lane to refuse them: it reads the session itself, so from the moment the session ended, UserInfo and introspection refuse every access token of it, an at+jwt included by its sid, and the token endpoint refuses its refresh tokens and its codes not exchanged yet with invalid_grant. A refresh token of the session presented before the lane has revoked it is the act refresh_token.outlived_its_session, and so is a successor a refresh in flight saved after that revocation, which is revoked in turn. Once revoked, a refresh token is refused as any revoked token is, and that refusal writes nothing.

    What the provider cannot reach is a self-contained access token in the hands of a resource server that verifies it offline (RFC 9068 §4): it is accepted there until it expires, ten minutes after it was issued unless tokens.access_token_ttl says otherwise. A resource server that has to know at once introspects, or its clients are declared with opaque access tokens.

    A session is shared with SAML. The session a client signed in under is the one the SAML service providers of the browser signed in under too, which is why the end session endpoint goes through them before it reaches the post_logout_redirect_uri, and why a LogoutRequest of a service provider ends the session of the clients of OpenID Connect as well. Single logout says how that chain is played.

    What a client registers¶

    Member What it is for
    post_logout_redirect_uris Where a logout may send the browser back to, compared byte for byte (RP-Initiated Logout 1.0 §3)
    frontchannel_logout_uri Loaded in a frame of the front-channel page when a session the client signed in under ends. HTTPS, or plain HTTP on a loopback host
    frontchannel_logout_session_required iss and sid added to that URI. It needs a frontchannel_logout_uri. Default false
    backchannel_logout_uri Where the logout token is posted. HTTPS, or plain HTTP on a loopback host
    backchannel_logout_session_required Whether the client reads the sid of the logout token, which is sent either way. It needs a backchannel_logout_uri. Default false
    config/packages/beffroi.yaml
    beffroi:
    clients:
        portal:
            # ...
            post_logout_redirect_uris: ['https://portal.example.com/signed-out']
            frontchannel_logout_uri: 'https://portal.example.com/logout/frontchannel'
            frontchannel_logout_session_required: true
            backchannel_logout_uri: 'https://portal.example.com/logout/backchannel'

    What a logout can promise¶

    • A session ended without its browser tells its clients only by the back channel. The console, the page of the account read on another browser, the other sessions closed or a password changed, a new person signing in: no frame is loaded and no SAML service provider is told, SOAP being out of scope.
    • The front channel reaches the clients the browser lets it reach, and says so.
    • A logout token is good for two hundred and thirty-five seconds by default: a client the provider cannot reach within that time is left believing its user signed in, and a client that refuses the token is written down as given up on.
    • A self-contained access token outlives the session at a resource server that verifies it offline, up to its expiry; the provider refuses it from the end of the session.
    • A session that expires is not a session that ends: nothing is announced for it.
