    title: Dynamic registration description: A client the provider has never seen, RFC 7591 and RFC 7592: closed by default, the three modes a tenant opens it in, what a registration may and may not ask for, and how a client reads, replaces and deletes itself.

    Dynamic registration¶

    A client may register itself at /register (RFC 7591), and then read, replace and delete its own registration at the URI it was handed (RFC 7592). Registration is closed by default: a tenant opens it by naming a mode, which decides what authorises a registration. A client that registers itself is read by the reader a declared client is read by, under the same rules, and is held besides to what its tenant offers.

    Opening it¶

    config/packages/beffroi.yaml
    beffroi:
    client_registration:
        mode: initial_access_token
        default_scope: 'openid profile email'
        keep_a_client_unused_for: 7776000
        keep_a_client_never_used_for: 604800
    Option Default What it decides
    mode none What authorises a registration: initial_access_token, software_statement or open. Left out, or empty, the tenant registers nobody and announces no registration endpoint
    default_scope none What a client that names no scope is registered with (RFC 7591 §2). Left out, openid alone. It is held to what the tenant hands out like every registered scope, so a scope written here that the tenant does not offer refuses every registration that names no scope
    exempt_confidential_clients_from_pkce false The confidential clients this policy registers are registered without the PKCE requirement. A public client is never exempted, and a challenge an exempted client sends is verified all the same
    keep_a_client_unused_for 7776000 Seconds a client that was used may go unused before it is deleted, ninety days
    keep_a_client_never_used_for 604800 Seconds a client that was never used lives at all, seven days
    statement_issuers none The authorities whose software statements the tenant accepts, for the software_statement mode
    statement_max_lifetime 300 Seconds ahead of the request the exp of a software statement may be
    statement_leeway 30 Seconds allowed of the clock of an authority, both ways
    sector_identifier_timeout 3.0 Seconds one read of a sector_identifier_uri is given, connection included
    sector_identifier_max_size 65536 Bytes a sector_identifier_uri may answer before the read stops

    Closed is the absence of the endpoint, not a mode that refuses. A tenant that names no mode answers a registration, a POST to /register, with a 404, as it answers a path it does not serve, and its discovery document has no registration_endpoint. A tenant that names a mode announces the endpoint, and its endpoint answers. /register takes a POST and no other method, whether the tenant registers or not. A mode nothing wired serves is a mistake of the deployment: the first registration fails on it, naming the modes that are wired.

    The three modes¶

    mode: initial_access_token registers a client presented by an initial access token the tenant issued (RFC 7591 §1.2): a credential for the team that integrates, named, worth a number of registrations and no more, expiring, and revocable. It opens no other endpoint, carries no subject and no scope, and says one thing only: that whoever holds it may register a client with this tenant.

    POST /register HTTP/1.1
Host: op.example.com
Content-Type: application/json
Authorization: Bearer 0NM8Z6ijkVrFDzEaMPwCLWmTvdFxNsRwBCzu8UxS4pQ

{"client_name": "Billing", "redirect_uris": ["https://billing.example.com/oidc/callback"]}

    The token is spent in the request it authorises, before the metadata is read: a token worth one registration registers one, whether the registration that followed was written or refused. A token unknown, spent, expired or revoked is answered exactly as a request that carries none, so that whoever does not hold a token of this tenant learns nothing about the one they presented.

    bin/console beffroi:client:token:issue 'Billing team' --registrations=1 --days=30
bin/console beffroi:client:token:list
bin/console beffroi:client:token:revoke 0192f1a4-6c1e-7b8a-9c3d-2e4f5a6b7c8d

    beffroi:client:token:issue prints the value once, and the provider keeps its SHA-256 and never the value: a token that was lost is replaced by issuing another. It is worth one registration and thirty days unless the command says otherwise, a day at least. beffroi:client:token:list shows what each token has served of what it is worth, and whether it still authorises anything. beffroi:client:token:revoke takes one back at once, and the clients it registered stay.

    mode: software_statement registers a client presented by a statement an authority the tenant pins signed (RFC 7591 §2.3), in the software_statement member of the body. The provider issues nothing: the proof is the authority's, which is what lets a registration cross the boundary of an organisation.

    config/packages/beffroi.yaml
    beffroi:
    client_registration:
        mode: software_statement
        statement_issuers:
            'https://authority.example.org':
                jwks: '%env(json:AUTHORITY_JWKS)%'
                signing_algorithms: ['ES256']

    An authority is keyed by the iss it signs with, an absolute URI compared byte for byte, and pins its public keys inline and the algorithms it signs with, among those the provider verifies. The key comes from this configuration and never from the statement: a key or a jwks_uri the statement names is never read (RFC 8725 §3.10). A tenant that pins no authority accepts no statement.

    The statement is verified before any of it is read as metadata. Its signature is checked against the keys of the authority its iss names, under one of the algorithms pinned for it. Its aud names the issuer of the tenant, once. Its exp is present, not past, and no further ahead than statement_max_lifetime. Its iat is present and not in the future. Its jti is present and never seen before, remembered until the exp, so one statement registers one client. RFC 7591 §2.3 asks none of this of a statement; the provider asks it of every signed thing it takes back. Whatever fails, the answer is the same invalid_software_statement.

    What a statement says outranks what the client sent (RFC 7591 §3.1.1): a member both carry is the statement's, so the registration is written with the client_name and the logo_uri the authority signed. A statement is held to what the tenant offers as a body is: one that names a scope, a grant or a method the tenant does not offer is refused with invalid_client_metadata, as a body naming it would be. The claims of RFC 7519 §4.1 of the statement describe the statement and are not metadata. A replacement of the registration reads no statement.

    The statement is the credential of this mode, and it travels in the body: a body that is not a JSON object is refused with invalid_client_metadata before anything else is read, and a JSON object that carries no statement is answered 401.

    mode: open registers whoever asks, with no credential at all (RFC 7591 §1.3): nothing authorises, so nothing is read and nothing is refused before the metadata is, and everything else, the reader, the policy and the refusals, is the same. A credential presented anyway is read by nothing and spent by nothing.

    A client nobody vouched for chooses the name and the logo a person reads on the consent page, and a tenant that names this mode for its real users lets anybody write them. The standalone application writes a client_registration in its development environment alone, where the mode is open unless the environment names another, for the conformance suite to register its clients.

    The request¶

    POST /register HTTP/1.1
Host: op.example.com
Content-Type: application/json
Authorization: Bearer 0NM8Z6ijkVrFDzEaMPwCLWmTvdFxNsRwBCzu8UxS4pQ

{
    "client_name": "Billing",
    "redirect_uris": ["https://billing.example.com/oidc/callback"],
    "token_endpoint_auth_method": "private_key_jwt",
    "jwks_uri": "https://billing.example.com/.well-known/jwks.json",
    "scope": "openid profile email"
}

    The body is one JSON object whose members are the metadata of the client, the members clients lists, read by the same reader under the same rules. A body that is not a JSON object carries no metadata and is refused with invalid_client_metadata: in the initial_access_token mode once the token has been read, and in the software_statement mode before anything, the statement being in the body.

    What a client may register is what the specifications define, and what the tenant offers:

    • The members this provider defines for itself are not a client's to send: requires_consent, requires_pkce, access_token_format, resources, token_exchange, may_act and require_sender_constrained_tokens are exceptions and privileges. A body that carries one is refused, and a software statement does not grant one either: a client that registers itself never obtains them.
    • A member nobody reads is refused, client_id and client_secret included: the provider draws both.
    • What a member names is one the provider is wired with: a grant, a method, an algorithm, a mode of delivery, a kind of subject, a type of authorization detail.
    • A scope is one the tenant hands out, the ones its discovery document lists in scopes_supported.
    • A sector_identifier_uri is proven: the provider reads the document behind it once, with one GET, no redirect followed, within sector_identifier_timeout and sector_identifier_max_size, and the document is the JSON array of URIs of OIDC Registration §5 that lists every redirect URI of the client, or its jwks_uri when it registers no redirect URI (CIBA Core §4). A document that cannot be read, or that leaves one out, refuses the registration (pairwise subjects).

    What a client leaves out takes the defaults of RFC 7591 §2: the authorization_code grant alone, the code response type that goes with it, and client_secret_basic. A scope left out is the default_scope of the tenant, or openid alone. The registration policy writes beside the registration what it grants, the default scope on a registration that named none and the PKCE exemption on a confidential client when the tenant grants it, and that is read back before what the client sent, until the policy writes the registration again.

    The answer¶

    HTTP/1.1 201 Created
Content-Type: application/json
Cache-Control: no-cache, no-store
Pragma: no-cache

{
    "client_id": "s6Bh3dRkqt3Hm9vBkrnfeR7_xU2ZyQ4lJaN0cP8TwYg",
    "client_id_issued_at": 1793001600,
    "registration_access_token": "Yx9o3Wq1fPz7kL2mN8rT4vB6cD0eF5gH1jK3lM9nQ2s",
    "registration_client_uri": "https://op.example.com/register/s6Bh3dRkqt3Hm9vBkrnfeR7_xU2ZyQ4lJaN0cP8TwYg",
    "client_name": "Billing",
    "redirect_uris": ["https://billing.example.com/oidc/callback"],
    "token_endpoint_auth_method": "private_key_jwt",
    "jwks_uri": "https://billing.example.com/.well-known/jwks.json",
    "scope": "openid profile email"
}

    The client_id is drawn by the provider, 256 random bits in base64url, and a client cannot choose it. When the method the client registered proves a secret, client_secret_basic, client_secret_post or client_secret_jwt, the answer also carries a client_secret drawn the same way and client_secret_expires_at at 0, which says the secret does not expire (RFC 7591 §3.2.1). The metadata follow, as the client sent them, a member a statement signed standing in place of the one the client sent.

    A secret and a registration access token are said once. The provider keeps a hash of the secret, or the secret sealed for client_secret_jwt, and the SHA-256 of the token, and answers neither again: a read of the registration carries neither.

    The registration is written before the answer is sent, so the client leaves with an identifier that exists.

    Reading, replacing and deleting itself¶

    The registration_client_uri answers the three methods of RFC 7592 §2, with the registration access token as a bearer credential and no other:

    Request Answer
    GET 200, the registration as the provider holds it, without the secret and without the token
    PUT 200, the registration replaced whole (RFC 7592 §2.2)
    DELETE 204, and the client is gone
    Another method 405

    A replacement is whole and never a patch: a member the request leaves out is a member the client no longer registers. The replacement is read by the reader, under the policy of the tenant and the rules a registration body is read under, so it names nothing a registration body could not; it is read from the request alone, whatever the mode, with no statement in front of it. The request names the client in client_id, the one the URI answers for; it carries none of client_id_issued_at, client_secret_expires_at, registration_access_token and registration_client_uri, which the provider assigned; and a client_secret it echoes is the one it holds. What the provider assigned stays: the identifier, the date and the token, which is not rotated. The secret is the one thing that may change: a client that registers a method proving a secret where it held none, or one its kept secret does not suit, is handed a new one, said once, and a client that registers a method proving none keeps no secret.

    A deleted client is gone at once, with its token: a request that presents it afterwards is answered as a token nobody holds. What the client was issued, consents, grants and refresh tokens, is left where it is and can no longer be spent, since nothing resolves the client it names. The sessions of the people who used it are not touched.

    A token that reads no client, a value nobody was handed, one drawn for another client, a client of another tenant or a client that deleted itself, is answered 401 with invalid_token and the same description, so the endpoint tells nobody which clients exist. The credential is read before the body: an unreadable body presented with a token that reads nothing is a 401, not a 400. These endpoints are not announced in the discovery document: a client holds the URI it was handed.

    The clients nobody uses¶

    A registered client carries the date it was last used, an authorization code issued for it or tokens issued to it at the token endpoint, and the provider deletes the ones nobody uses any more, on a timed step that runs every hour: a client used, but not for keep_a_client_unused_for, and a client never used and registered more than keep_a_client_never_used_for ago. One run deletes a hundred clients at most, and the next run takes the rest; what it deleted is written to the log. A tenant with no registration policy deletes nothing, and a declared client is never deleted: it goes away with its block in the configuration.

    Each deletion, by the step, by the client itself or by beffroi:client:delete, emits the same RegisteredClientDeleted event.

    Refusals¶

    Answer When
    404 A registration, at a tenant that registers nobody
    401, invalid_token, WWW-Authenticate: Bearer Nothing the request presents authorises it: no token, or one that authorises nothing; no statement in the software_statement mode; no registration access token of this client at its URI
    400, invalid_client_metadata A member that does not hold, is not read, is not the client's to send, names what the provider or the tenant does not offer, or a body that is not a JSON object
    400, invalid_redirect_uri A value of redirect_uris or post_logout_redirect_uris that is not an address a client may register, or the redirect URIs and the grants disagreeing. Every other address, and the rule the loopback addresses of a public client follow, answer invalid_client_metadata
    400, invalid_software_statement A statement the tenant does not accept, whatever the reason

    What these endpoints answer themselves, a registration, a read, a replacement, a deletion and their refusals, carries Cache-Control: no-cache, no-store and Pragma: no-cache. The description of a 400 is the sentence of the refusal, in ASCII (RFC 7591 §3.2.2), the same sentence a deployment reads when a declaration of the same member is refused.
