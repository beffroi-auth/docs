    Upstream identity providers¶

    The tenant can sign a person in against the SAML identity provider of their organisation rather than with a credential of its own. For that it is a service provider, to the identity providers the deployment declares upstream of it, each one answering for the e-mail domains it names. This page is the role and its peers; signing in through an organisation is what a person goes through.

    Turning it on¶

    config/packages/beffroi.yaml
    beffroi:
    saml:
        service_provider:
            enabled: true
    Option Default What it decides
    enabled false Whether the role exists. Off, nothing of it is wired, and its metadata, its assertion consumer service and its sign-on answer 404
    entity_id the URL of its metadata The entity ID of the service provider, for one its identity providers already know by a name it keeps: an absolute URI of 1024 characters at most, with no whitespace, control, format or invisible character

    The service provider signs with the SAML signing key of the identity provider, one key for both roles, rotated by the figures of beffroi.saml.identity_provider whichever role is on. A deployment that serves only this role draws that key the same way:

    bin/console beffroi:keys:generate --algorithm=SAML-SIGNING

    The two roles are two entities. The entity ID of the service provider is never the one of the identity provider of the tenant, which the configuration refuses whether that role is on or not, so turning it on later never makes the two one.

    Its metadata¶

    Served at /saml/sp/metadata, which is also its entity ID unless entity_id names another. They hold:

    • one md:SPSSODescriptor of SAML 2.0, with AuthnRequestsSigned="true", since every request it sends is signed, and WantAssertionsSigned="true", so that an identity provider configured from them signs its assertion;
    • one KeyDescriptor use="signing" per SAML key in service, as the identity provider's metadata list them;
    • one assertion consumer service, on HTTP-POST, at /saml/sp/acs;
    • the cacheDuration of the identity provider's metadata, and no validUntil.

    They list no single logout service, single logout with an upstream identity provider not being served, and no key for encryption: the certificate an identity provider encrypts for is published to that provider alone, below. Like the identity provider's, they are unsigned, and every URL in them comes from the configuration of the tenant.

    Declaring an identity provider¶

    config/packages/beffroi.yaml
    beffroi:
    saml:
        service_provider: true
        identity_providers:
            'https://idp.example.org/saml/metadata':
                metadata: 'https://idp.example.org/saml/metadata'
                domains: ['example.org']
                email_attribute: mail
                profile:
                    givenName: given_name
                    sn: family_name
                groups:
                    attribute: memberOf
                    map:
                        staff: Staff
    Option Default What it decides
    The key The entity ID of the identity provider, compared exactly with the one of its metadata. Never an entity ID of the tenant itself
    metadata required Where its metadata are read from: an https URL, no redirect followed, or the absolute path of a file of the deployment
    metadata_signers none The certificates its metadata are signed by, each the base64 of its DER. Named, the metadata are verified under them and must carry a validUntil
    domains required The e-mail domains it answers for, one at least and 256 at most
    email_attribute required The name of the attribute that carries the e-mail address of a person
    profile none The attributes that fill the profile of a person, keyed by the name of the attribute, each naming the claim it fills
    subject_attribute none The attribute that names a person when the NameID of an assertion is not persistent
    groups none The attribute that carries the groups of a person, and the group of the tenant each of its values stands for
    require_encrypted_assertions false An assertion arrives encrypted, or it is refused
    address_parameter none The parameter of its single sign-on service that takes the address the person typed, which it prefills its sign-in with

    A declaration is refused while the service provider of the tenant is not enabled, and one that does not hold together fails the build of the deployment, the claims of profile included, which are checked right after the container is compiled. A declaration holding an environment variable is held to the same rules at its first use.

    Domains. A domain is a name of the DNS, read without regard to case, a name outside ASCII written as its A-label. It is the whole part of an address after its @ and nothing under it: example.org does not answer for eu.example.org, which a provider that answers for it declares too. A domain answers to one identity provider of the tenant, and a domain two of them declare is refused, naming it and both providers: which provider answers for an address is never a choice.

    The profile. One attribute per claim, among the claims a person can describe themselves with in this deployment: name, given_name, family_name, middle_name, nickname, preferred_username, birthdate, gender, locale, zoneinfo, profile, picture, website and phone_number, or whatever claims the deployment wires. email is not among them, since email_attribute carries it. A telephone number is written unverified: the provider filling it is not a proof that anybody verified it.

    The subject. A person is named at their identity provider by a persistent NameID, a value it keeps for them. For an identity provider that sends another format, subject_attribute names the attribute that does the same job: a value that never changes, such as the object identifier Microsoft Entra ID sends, and never an address. The attribute of the e-mail is refused there, since an address is given again to somebody else, and the person hired under the address of one who left would be signed in to the account of that person.

    Groups. Each value of the attribute is compared exactly with the keys of map, 256 values at most, and a value the map does not name is no group. The groups the map names are groups of the tenant, found by their name, and none is ever created from what an identity provider sends: create them first.

    bin/console beffroi:group:create Staff

    Its metadata, read and kept¶

    Exactly as a service provider's are, by the same objects: read from the declared location, verified under pinned signers or trusted by their channel, refused when expired, never older than the document accepted when they are signed, sealed where they sit, and refreshed every hour by the timed step. They are refused besides when they hold no md:IDPSSODescriptor of SAML 2.0, no key for signing, or a single sign-on service that is not https on a host.

    bin/console beffroi:saml:identity-provider:refresh
bin/console beffroi:saml:identity-provider:forget 'https://idp.example.org/saml/metadata'

    An identity provider whose metadata were never accepted cannot be signed in through: read them before announcing the domain to anybody.

    The key it encrypts for¶

    An identity provider that encrypts its assertions encrypts them for a key of the tenant, and each identity provider has one of its own, never shared with another nor with the signing key. Its algorithm names the provider:

    bin/console beffroi:keys:generate --algorithm='SAML-ENCRYPTION:https://idp.example.org/saml/metadata'

    It is an RSA key of the tenant like the others, sealed by the key management service, absent from the JWK Set so that no OpenID Connect client encrypts to it, and rotated with the commands and the timed step of every key, by the figures of the SAML signing key: seven days staged, a year of work.

    Its certificate goes to that provider alone. The metadata of the service provider for one identity provider are served at /saml/sp/metadata/ followed by the entity ID of that provider in base64url, without padding (RFC 4648 §5):

    printf '%s' 'https://idp.example.org/saml/metadata' | basenc --base64url --wrap=0 | tr -d '='

    They are the metadata above, with one KeyDescriptor use="encryption" per key of that provider staged or active, the newest first, and none of another provider. An identity provider takes the first certificate it finds, so listing the staged key first is what makes it encrypt for the next key days before that key takes over; the retiring key is no longer announced, and still opens what was encrypted for it until it retires. The certificate is self-signed, its one extension keyUsage with keyEncipherment alone. The URL answers 404 while the tenant holds no key in service for that provider.

    A ciphertext of a provider is opened by the keys of that provider, and by no other. The keys tried are those of the identity provider whose metadata verified the response, its staged, active and retiring ones in turn; the KeyInfo of the encrypted key is not read. A key shared between two providers would let one of them carry the other's ciphertext under its own signature and read what the decryption answers, which is why there is one key per provider.

    What arrives What happens
    AES-GCM, its key under RSA-OAEP Decrypted
    AES-CBC, in a response whose own signature verifies Decrypted, the signature covering the ciphertext. Entra, AD FS and SimpleSAMLphp encrypt under CBC
    AES-CBC, in a response that is not signed Refused by name: CBC authenticates nothing, so it is opened only under a signature
    An encrypted assertion from a provider the tenant holds no key for Refused by name
    An assertion in clear from a provider declared with require_encrypted_assertions: true Refused before anything of it is read

    Every cryptographic failure of a decryption is one and the same refusal, so that nothing in the answer tells a padding from a key.
