    Passkeys¶

    A passkey is a discoverable WebAuthn credential (W3C Web Authentication Level 3), enrolled from the pages of the account or from an enrolment ticket, and used on its own, with no password in the way. A person holds as many as they enrol, each with a name they gave it. The provider holds the public key, the counters and the flags of a passkey, and never anything it could sign with: the private key does not leave the authenticator.

    A passkey proves the possession of a key, and it is what lets a client ask for a phishing-resistant authentication: under the default policy of a tenant, a session a passkey opened answers phr, or phrh for a passkey bound to its authenticator (the classes it answers with).

    The relying party¶

    Parameter Value
    Relying party identifier The host of the issuer (WebAuthn Level 3 §5.4.2)
    Origin accepted The origin of the issuer, scheme and port included, so plain HTTP on a loopback host only, as for the issuer
    Algorithms ES256 and RS256, COSE -7 and -257 (RFC 9053 §2.1, RFC 8812 §2), in that order of preference
    Challenge 32 random bytes, drawn for each ceremony (§13.4.3 asks 16 at the least)
    Resident key Required: a passkey is a discoverable credential (§5.4.6)
    User verification Preferred and never required, so that a security key with no PIN enrols and signs in
    Attestation None asked (§5.4.7)

    Enrolling one¶

    On the page of their passkeys, /account/passkeys, a person adds one in a click: the page asks for creation options, the browser creates the passkey, and the page posts it back, named New passkey in the language of the page, a name they correct in its line afterwards. The options are kept in the session of that browser until the passkey comes back, and the page asks for a proof made in the last five minutes before it lets a passkey be added.

    • The user is named by a handle and not by an identifier. The user handle of §5.4.3 is 64 random bytes, drawn for the first passkey of a person and shared by all of their passkeys, never the subject and never the address (§14.6.1). The user entity carries the username as its name, and the name claim, or the username, as what the authenticator shows.
    • Every passkey the person holds is excluded, revoked or not, so an authenticator does not enrol twice.
    • What comes back is checked against the options: the challenge, the origin and the relying party, then the record. A credential ID the tenant already holds is refused (§7.1, step 26), so are options drawn for another person, a record without its backup flags and a credential of another type than public-key.
    • An attestation an authenticator sends anyway is verified, in the formats packed, fido-u2f, android-key, tpm and apple. none is what is asked, and any other format is a refusal and never a pass. The attestation is not kept, and no policy reads it afterwards.
    • The floor of the tenant is asked once the passkey is known. What a passkey proves is read from the flags of its creation, so a tenant whose floor is phishing_resistant_hardware refuses a synced passkey once the ceremony has answered, and admits one bound to its authenticator.

    A passkey enrolled writes the act credential.enrolled to the journal of the person.

    Signing in with one¶

    The login page asks the browser for a discoverable credential: no username and no list of allowed passkeys, so the page says nothing of who holds one. The options are drawn for that browser and kept in its session until the assertion comes back.

    The assertion is verified against the passkey of the tenant its credential ID names, the signature first. Then, so that a refusal costs what a wrong signature costs, the provider refuses a passkey of another tenant, an assertion whose backup eligibility is not the one of the enrolment, a revoked passkey, a person of a federated domain and a disabled person.

    What the assertion proves When
    hwk Always, for a passkey bound to its authenticator
    swk Always, for a synced passkey
    user The authenticator tested the presence of the user, the UP flag of its authenticator data (§6.1)
    mfa The authenticator verified the user, with a PIN or a biometric it does not name, the UV flag (§6.1)

    Under a floor of two_factors, a passkey proves two factors only when the authenticator verified the user: a security key without a PIN proves the possession of a key, one factor, and that login is refused.

    The counters are read, not trusted. The sign count and the backup state of the assertion replace the stored ones (§7.2, step 26). When either count is not zero, a count that did not grow is told, by the event passkey.used that announces the login, and not refused, a synced passkey keeping its count at zero.

    Synced or bound¶

    Whether a passkey may be synced to other devices is the backup eligibility flag its authenticator set when it was created, which WebAuthn Level 3 §6.1.3 forbids to change. The flag recorded at the enrolment is the one the provider reads for the rest of the life of that passkey: it decides whether a login with it proves swk or hwk, and so, under the default policy, whether the session answers phr or phrh. An assertion that carries another flag is refused, as a revoked passkey is, rather than rewriting the record.

    A first passkey from a ticket¶

    A person created on a tenant that signs its people in by passkey has nothing to sign in with, and no password to be given. An administrator issues them a ticket instead:

    bin/console beffroi:user:enrolment-ticket alice

    The command prints a link, /enrol/{ticket}/{value} under the issuer, once, with the instant it opens until, and the row keeps a hash of what the link carries, so a store read by somebody else opens nothing. Whoever issued it hands it over.

    • It opens one enrolment, once. The page asks the browser for a passkey, and the ticket is spent only when the passkey is in the rows: a ceremony that could not finish leaves the person their link.
    • For seventy-two hours, which covers a weekend between the day it is issued and the day somebody starts. What is not used in three days is issued again.
    • One at a time. Issuing a ticket forgets the unspent ones of that person, so a link forgotten in a mailbox stops opening anything the day another is issued. A disabled account gets none.
    • It proves no login. Once the passkey is posed, the browser is sent to the account, which sends it to sign in with that passkey: the session comes from the passkey, never from possession of the link.

    A link that opens nothing, an identifier nobody issued, a wrong value, a ticket spent, out of time or of a disabled person, is answered by the same page with the status 404, and each of them costs one hash, so neither the answer nor its time says which it was. An identifier that is not a UUID is no ticket at all, and is answered 404 without a hash. The passkey the ticket poses is enrolled under the same rules as any other, the floor of the tenant included. The page needs a browser that can hold a passkey, and says so to one that cannot. Issuing a ticket is an act of the journal of that person, enrolment_ticket.issued.

    Renaming and revoking¶

    A person renames a passkey in its line on the page of their passkeys, 1 to 255 characters once the whitespace around it is trimmed, and the event credential.renamed carries the name it had. A revoked passkey is a record of what was: its line offers no field to rename it.

    Revoking one is asked for a proof made in the last five minutes, since it is the gesture that can leave somebody with no way in at all. The passkey never authenticates again and its line stays, listed as revoked with its date; the sessions it opened are left as they are. An operator revokes one with beffroi:user:revoke-credential, which credentials describes.

    Not for the people of an organisation¶

    A person whose address is of a domain an upstream identity provider answers for signs in there, and is posed no passkey here: the pages of the account offer them no form, an enrolment, a ticket's included, is refused, and a passkey they held before their domain was federated is refused at the login.

    The FIDO metadata¶

    The metadata of the FIDO Alliance say, authenticator by authenticator, what it is and whether it was found compromised. Two commands store them under beffroi.webauthn.metadata_storage, var/webauthn of the project by default, and nothing refreshes them on its own:

    bin/console beffroi:webauthn:mds-refresh
bin/console beffroi:webauthn:mds-import vendor-statement.json
    Command What it stores
    beffroi:webauthn:mds-refresh The Metadata BLOB of https://mds.fidoalliance.org/, or of each --url given, verified before anything is kept: one signature, made by the first certificate of its chain, the chain leading to the root it pins, GlobalSign Root CA - R3 shipped with the bundle unless --root names another, as a PEM or DER file or an https URL. No redirect is followed, and a refused blob keeps nothing
    beffroi:webauthn:mds-import Metadata statements a manufacturer publishes for keys the service does not list, or that the FIDO conformance tools ship, one authenticator per file, unsigned and trusted as the file is

    What reads them is a ceremony whose options ask for an attestation: an authenticator its status reports name as compromised is refused, a full attestation is checked up to the root its statement names, and an AAGUID the metadata do not know is refused on a full attestation and passed on a self attestation. The enrolment of the provider asks for no attestation, so a deployment that never ran either command enrols passkeys all the same.
