    title: Users description: A person of a tenant: the username, the subject clients know them by, the claims of their profile, and the source that created them.

    Users¶

    A user belongs to a tenant, is named by a username unique within it, and is known to clients by a subject identifier that never changes, even when the username or the address does. The standard claims of OIDC Core §5.1 are held beside the identity, each one a field of its own in the model rather than an entry in a bag of strings, and the person names the source that created them. Their credentials, their linked identities and their group memberships hang off the user.

    What a user holds¶

    Field What it holds
    Subject The sub of OIDC Core §2: 1 to 255 printable ASCII characters, no space. Unique within the tenant, never changed and never given to anybody else
    Username What the person types to name themselves: 1 to 255 bytes once the whitespace around it is trimmed, unique within the tenant and matched exactly, case included
    Email address Optional, a valid address, with a flag that says whether it was verified. An absent address cannot be verified
    Phone number Optional, with a flag of its own that says whether it was verified
    Address The structured claim of OIDC Core §5.1.1. An address with no member is no address, and is not held
    Profile The thirteen other claims of OIDC Core §5.1, below
    Source Local, or the upstream provider that created the person and the claims it holds of them
    Dates When the record was created, when it last changed, and when the account was disabled, if it was

    The subject stays readable in the database, a stable pseudonym that names a person without saying anything about them; every other field of the person that says something about them is sealed under the data key of the tenant. Personal data says how.

    The subject clients know them by¶

    The subject is drawn when the user is created, a random UUID (version 4, RFC 9562 §5.4) that says nothing of when the account was opened. An import brings its own instead, so that the clients of the provider it came from find the same person under the same sub:

    bin/console beffroi:user:create alice --subject 248289761001

    Nothing changes it afterwards: a new username or a new address leaves it as it was. It is the sub a client receives, unless that client is registered for a pairwise identifier (OIDC Core §8.1), in which case it receives one derived for its sector, and the pairwise subjects page says how. The pages of the account show neither.

    Creating one¶

    bin/console beffroi:user:create alice --email alice@example.com
    Argument or option What it says
    username What the user types to name themselves, unique in the tenant
    --email Their address, unverified
    --email-verified The address was verified by whoever provisions the user
    --subject The sub of an identity being imported, drawn otherwise

    A username or a subject the tenant already holds is refused, and so is an address or a subject that is not well formed. The command prints the username, the subject, the email address with its state, the identifier of the row and the slug of the tenant. It gives the user no credential: a password is beffroi:user:set-password, and a first passkey on a tenant that holds no password is an enrolment ticket.

    A person is also created by an upstream identity provider, at their first sign-in through their organisation. That person is named by their address, which is their username, the address is verified, and the source says which provider created them.

    The claims of the profile¶

    Claim What it holds
    name, given_name, family_name, middle_name, nickname, preferred_username Names
    profile, picture, website Three URLs
    gender A free string, OIDC Core §5.1 naming two values and allowing others
    birthdate YYYY-MM-DD, or YYYY alone, 0000 standing for a year nobody knows. A value of another shape is refused, wherever it comes from; the shape is what is checked, four digits and two pairs of two, and not whether that month and that day exist
    zoneinfo The clock of the person, a name of the zoneinfo database
    locale The language the person reads
    email, email_verified The address and its flag
    phone_number, phone_number_verified The number and its flag
    address The six members of OIDC Core §5.1.1: formatted, street_address, locality, region, postal_code, country

    A claim that is not set is absent. The provider holds nothing under that name, and a client is never sent the claim with a null value: it is sent no claim at all. A flag goes with its value, so email_verified is sent only beside an address and phone_number_verified only beside a number, and an address with no member is no address. updated_at, the instant the record last changed, always has a value. Which claims a client receives, by scope or by name, is scopes and claims.

    Describing a user from the console¶

    bin/console beffroi:user:profile alice --given-name Alice --family-name Liddell --locale fr-FR

    It writes the whole profile. What the command does not say is cleared, so a run says every claim the person is to keep. A run that says nothing is refused rather than taken for a request to clear everything, and a value left empty says nothing. The command prints the name, the given name, the family name, the phone number and the postal address it wrote; reading a user without writing is beffroi:user:show.

    Claim Option
    name, given_name, family_name, middle_name --name, --given-name, --family-name, --middle-name
    nickname, preferred_username --nickname, --preferred-username
    profile, picture, website --profile-url, --picture, --website (--profile is an option of the console itself)
    gender, birthdate, zoneinfo, locale --gender, --birthdate, --zoneinfo, --locale
    phone_number, phone_number_verified --phone-number, --phone-number-verified
    address --address for formatted, --street-address, --locality, --region, --postal-code, --country

    The email address is not among them: it is given when the user is created, and rewritten afterwards only by the upstream provider that created the person. No command renames a user either.

    What a person says of themselves¶

    A person changes, on the page of their account, the claims their tenant opens in tenant.self_served_claims, and no other: a tenant that opens none serves a page that only reads. Fourteen claims can be opened: name, given_name, family_name, middle_name, nickname, preferred_username, birthdate, gender, locale, zoneinfo, profile, picture, website and phone_number, and a name none of them answers refuses the start. The rule is applied by the use case that writes, for every caller, and not only by the page that shows the form.

    • A closed set is closed on the server. Described by the person, zoneinfo takes a name of the zoneinfo database this PHP carries and locale one of the locales its ICU data holds, written as a BCP 47 tag, and anything else is refused; a PHP whose ICU holds no data closes no set for locale. beffroi:user:profile holds neither claim to these sets.
    • A new number is not a verified one. A person who writes a telephone number takes back its verification, since what was proved was proved of the number that is gone.
    • What it said before is kept. Each change writes what the claim said and what it says now, sealed, kept for beffroi.history.retention days, 180 by default, and read at /account/profile/changes. The act, person.described_themselves, goes to the journal of the person, which keeps the act and never the value. Saying again what a claim already says writes the act and no change.

    Choosing a language on a page writes the locale claim of a signed-in person, and that claim alone.

    The source that created a person¶

    A person names the source that created them. Local, for a person the console, a fixture or an import created; or an upstream provider, for a person created at their first sign-in through their organisation, with the claims of their profile that provider fills.

    What a source holds, a manual description does not change. beffroi:user:profile refuses a held claim said otherwise, naming the provider, before anything is written; it keeps a held claim it is not told about rather than clearing it, and it accepts one said with the value it already has, so a provisioning script can run again. The page of the account offers the person no field for a held claim, and the use case behind it refuses one posted by hand. A choice of language on a page leaves a held locale as the provider wrote it.

    What the provider stops saying is released. At every sign-in through it, the source holds the address, the telephone number when the provider said one, and those of the claims it fills that it said at that sign-in, and nothing else: a claim it no longer says is left with the value it wrote last, and from then on the person, or the console, may change it.

    The username follows the address. A person a provider created is named by their address, and when the provider sends another one, the username becomes that address in the same unit as the rest of the sign-in.

    beffroi:user:show prints the source beside the identity: local, or the name of the provider followed by the claims it holds. The source is sealed with the rest of the person, since the provider that created somebody says which organisation they are of.

    Reading a user¶

    bin/console beffroi:user:show alice

    It prints the username, the subject, whether the account is enabled, when it was created, the email address with its state and the source, then the name, the given name, the family name, the phone number and the postal address. Below them, one line per credential with its kind, identifier, name and dates, revoked ones included, and one line per group with the source of the membership and the date it was added. It writes nothing. The console commands list what else reads a person: beffroi:user:sessions, beffroi:user:journal and beffroi:user:applications.

    A disabled user¶

    An account carries the date it was disabled, and a disabled person is refused at these places:

    • every login, by password, by passkey or by code, after the credential was checked, so that the refusal costs what a wrong credential costs and says nothing of the state of the account;
    • a sign-in through an upstream provider, before anything the provider said is written;
    • an enrolment ticket, which is not issued for them and no longer opens anything once they are;
    • an assertion of a trusted issuer at the token endpoint (RFC 7523 §3) that names them;
    • an access token that names them, at UserInfo, at introspection, and presented to the token exchange (RFC 8693).

    Nothing else reads the state of the account: a session already open goes on serving the browser that holds it, and a refresh token goes on being exchanged at the token endpoint. beffroi:user:show says disabled since and the date. No command or page of the bundle disables or enables an account.
