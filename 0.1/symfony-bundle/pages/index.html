    Pages¶

    The bundle ships the pages a person reads as Twig templates in the @Beffroi namespace, translated, and overridable like any template of any bundle. They carry the structure and the words, never a stylesheet: an application gives them their look by overriding one file, and any page it wants otherwise by its name. The scripts they load are the bundle's own, served by the bundle under the path of its routes, so a deployment has nothing to build for them; the pages that post a form carry one line of script of their own, named by its hash.

    Making them yours¶

    A template of the bundle is overridden by a file of the same name under templates/bundles/BeffroiBundle/ of the application. Every page extends layout.html.twig but the four that carry a step of the protocol, so overriding that one file gives every page its look, and the pages come with it untouched:

    templates/bundles/BeffroiBundle/layout.html.twig
    <!DOCTYPE html>
<html lang="{{ app.request.locale|replace({'_': '-'}) }}">
<head>
    <meta charset="utf-8">
    <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1">
    <title>{% block title %}{% endblock %}</title>
    <link rel="stylesheet" href="{{ asset('styles/app.css') }}">
    {% block head %}{% endblock %}
</head>
<body>
    {% block brand %}{% include '@Beffroi/_brand.html.twig' %}{% endblock %}
    {% block body %}
        <main>
            <h1>{% block heading %}{{ block('title') }}{% endblock %}</h1>
            {% block action %}{% endblock %}
            {% block content %}{% endblock %}
        </main>
    {% endblock %}
    {% block locale %}{% include '@Beffroi/_locale_switcher.html.twig' %}{% endblock %}
</body>
</html>
    Block What the pages put in it
    title The title of the page, which heading repeats unless a page says otherwise
    head The script of the bundle a page needs: passkey.js on the login page, the page of passkeys and the enrolment by ticket, push.js on the page of notifications, copy.js where a value is handed over to copy, password-strength.js on the page of the password
    brand The logo of the tenant, from _brand.html.twig
    heading The heading of the page
    action The one action a page offers beside its title, when it offers one
    content The page itself
    locale The choice of a language, from _locale_switcher.html.twig

    A stylesheet goes outside head, as above: a page that loads a script fills that block, and its own block replaces the layout's. The standalone application draws the pages with Tailwind and DaisyUI this way, from a layout that picks one of two skeletons by the route, one for the pages of an interaction and one for the pages of the account. The error page sets beffroi_refusal, so a layout tells a refusal served on a route of the account from a page of the account.

    The functions and filters the templates read are the bundle's, for a page an application rewrites:

    Twig What it gives
    beffroi_tenant() What the page shows of its tenant: name, logoUri, primaryColor, primaryContentColor, or nothing when the row of the tenant is missing or disagrees with the configuration
    beffroi_locales() The languages the deployment serves, the default one first
    beffroi_locale_choice() The same list, or none when the record of a signed-in person decided the language of the page
    beffroi_moment A date, on the clock of the person, in the words of the language of the page, the zone always named
    beffroi_place, beffroi_device Where a request came from, and the browser it named

    What a tenant shows of itself¶

    The logo the tenant declared is on every page that keeps the brand block, with the name of the tenant as the text that stands in for it, so a reader who does not see the image is told who they came for. It is loaded by the browser of the visitor, never by the provider, and with no referrer. A tenant that declares no logo renders nothing there.

    The two colours the tenant declares, the one the buttons and the links are drawn in and the one of what is written on them, are read by the layout of the application, since the look of the pages is the application's: the bundle applies no colour. The standalone application writes them as two custom properties of a style element carrying the nonce of the response.

    The consent page shows the logo of the asking client beside the one of the tenant, and adds the origins both are served from to the img-src of the policy its answer carries. Every other page is served under the policy of the deployment alone, so that policy allows the origin the logo of the tenant is served from: the standalone application serves it from its own origin, which its img-src 'self' allows.

    The login page¶

    It offers what the tenant admits. It shows the logins the firewall wires that the floor of the tenant accepts: a tenant that signs its people in by passkey shows no password field and no username, the passkey naming its own user. A tenant that federates domains to upstream identity providers asks for the address first, one field and nothing else, and sends an address of those domains to its provider.

    The password form comes first. The passkey form is hidden until the script finds a browser that can hold a passkey, then shows a button, and the username field offers the passkeys the browser holds when it can (autocomplete="username webauthn"). A refused login shows one message, whatever the reason. On a step-up the page names the classes the client accepts and says which kinds of credential the person holds would reach one. A login asked for by a page of the provider names no client.

    The consent page¶

    Who is asking is what the client registered of itself: its name, its logo, and the pages a person may read before deciding, policy_uri, tos_uri and client_uri in that order, each opened with no referrer. Then the person signed in, every scope of the request, the standard scopes of OIDC Core §5.4 in words and any other by its value, and what the request asks for beyond its scopes (RFC 9396 §2) in the words of its type. Allowing ends with the code carried to the client, so the page adds the origin of its redirection URI to the form-action of its policy.

    The logout pages¶

    The logout asks for a confirmation when the request did not prove it is about the session the browser holds (OIDC RP-Initiated Logout 1.0 §2), and the form posts the same parameters back with the answer. After it, the page says the person is signed out, or still signed in when they chose to stay. When clients of the session registered a front-channel logout URI, the page that loads them in frames is served once the session has ended, and goes on to where the logout was going.

    The device pages¶

    The first page asks for the code a device shows, one field and nothing else, and one message when the code names no request awaiting an answer, whatever the reason. The second says what the code names: the provider that is asking, the person signed in, the client that asked and the code, where the device asked from, every scope of the request, and a warning that somebody who started nothing refuses. Nothing is approved on a GET: the answer is a post of that page.

    The pages that post a form¶

    Three pages carry a message of the protocol rather than a page of the product: the answer of the authorization endpoint in response_mode=form_post (OAuth 2.0 Form Post Response Mode §2), a SAML message to a peer, and a form another site posted without the session cookie, posted again from the provider's own origin. None of them extends the layout. Each carries the policy default-src 'none' and submits itself with the one script that policy names by its hash, and keeps a button for a browser that runs no script; their words are translated like every other page's. The page of the front-channel logout is built the same way, its policy naming the frames it opens.

    The script of these pages is their policy

    They can be overridden like any other, as long as the script is kept exactly as it is given: the hash in the Content-Security-Policy is the hash of that script, and a script that differs by a character does not run.

    The headers of a page¶

    Every page the bundle renders on the interaction plane carries these, and the pages that post a form carry their own policy in place of the floor:

    Header Value
    X-Frame-Options DENY
    Content-Security-Policy frame-ancestors 'none'
    Referrer-Policy no-referrer
    Cache-Control no-store; no-cache, no-store, with Pragma: no-cache, on the page that posts a SAML message and on the page that posts a form again

    frame-ancestors 'none' is a floor: it restricts framing and nothing else, and a deployment that wants a whole policy on these pages writes it, as the standalone application does. A page that has something to say about the request it answers never writes a policy of its own: it adds what it needs to whichever policy the answer carries, the enforced one and the one that only reports, once that policy is written.

    • The login page, the code page and the consent page add the origin of the redirection URI of the client to form-action, since a browser checks that directive over the whole chain of redirections of a submission, the last of which carries the code to the client. The logout confirmation adds the origin of the post-logout redirection URI, and the origin of the single logout service of each SAML service provider the logout goes through.
    • The consent page adds the origins of the two logos it shows to img-src.

    A policy that names no form-action is left alone, that directive falling back to no other; a policy that names default-src and no img-src gets an img-src written out of it, with the origins added.

    The scripts are served with Cache-Control: public for an hour and an ETag:

    Path Read by
    /passkey.js The login page, the enrolment of a passkey on the account and by ticket
    /push.js, /push-worker.js The page of notifications, and the service worker it registers
    /copy.js The pages that hand over a value to copy, the key of an authenticator application and the backup codes
    /password-strength.js The page of the password

    What an override keeps¶

    A page is free to move, reword or restyle anything, and three things are read by something else. The fields of a form, which a controller or an authenticator reads by name. The form tokens, each rendered under its identifier with csrf_token(). The data-* attributes the scripts read, and the ?interaction= the actions carry, or the interaction field of the consent form, which is how a post finds its interaction.

    Form Fields Token
    Password login _username, _password _csrf_token, authenticate
    Passkey login _passkey, in a form marked data-passkey="login" naming its options in data-options-url, with a data-passkey-button and a data-passkey-error _csrf_token, authenticate
    Second step _code _csrf_token, authenticate
    Address first _address _token, beffroi_login_address
    Consent interaction, answer with allow or deny _token, beffroi_consent
    Logout The parameters of the request, confirm with yes or no _token, beffroi_logout
    Device user_code, and answer with allow or deny on the second page _token, beffroi_device
    Language locale, target, and interaction when there is one _token, beffroi_locale
