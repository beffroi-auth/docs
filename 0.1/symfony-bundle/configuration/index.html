    Configuration¶

    The bundle reads one tree under beffroi, and each key at its root is a section with a class of its own in the bundle. Two sections are required, tenant and keys. Every other one has defaults a provider runs on, and a capability whose section names nothing is either served with those defaults or not served at all: a tenant that names no registration mode has no registration endpoint, in its discovery document as on the wire. This page goes through the sections in the order of the tree; the reference lists every option with its type, its default and its bounds.

    The smallest configuration names the issuer and the key management client the private keys are sealed with:

    config/packages/beffroi.yaml
    beffroi:
    tenant:
        issuer: '%env(BEFFROI_ISSUER)%'
    keys:
        kms: default
        key_id: '%env(BEFFROI_KMS_KEY_ID)%'

    The sections¶

    mutual_tls The host that asks a client for its certificate No alias announced

    When a mistake is caught¶

    When the container is built, at cache:clear, at cache:warmup, or at the first boot of a process that finds no compiled container. The tree is checked there: an unknown key at any level, a value of the wrong type, a number written outside its bounds, a required option missing, a value outside its list. So are the rules the tree reads between options, a sliding refresh lifetime longer than the absolute one, a SAML cache_duration that is not shorter than the SAML staging delay, a SAML peer declared for a role that is off; the name of the key management client; and every SAML declaration, the claims it names included. A deployment pipeline that warms its cache stops there. A SAML declaration that holds an environment variable is checked at its first use instead.

    When the provider first reads a declaration. What only the wiring can answer is checked against the wiring, the first time the provider builds the object: the tenant, each declared client, each trusted issuer, each authority of software statements, the default scope of registrations. A refusal names the option and, where the wiring decides, the names it is wired with, so a misspelt algorithm is answered with the algorithms that exist. What reads a declaration first is what meets its refusal:

    • bin/console beffroi:tenant:synchronize builds the tenant;
    • bin/console beffroi:client:list, once the tenant is synchronized, reads every declared client, and with one client declared at least, every trusted issuer as well;
    • the discovery document, /.well-known/openid-configuration under the issuer, builds all of it once the tenant is synchronized: the tenant, the declared clients, the trusted issuers, and the policy of registration, its authorities of software statements and its default scope included, which no command of the bundle builds.

    A deployment that runs beffroi:tenant:synchronize and then reads the discovery document has met every one of these refusals before a client asks anything. Otherwise the first request that needs the object meets it, and the policy of registration is also built by the timed step that removes the clients nobody uses.

    No option does it wrong. The tree holds no insecure_* key and no compatibility switch. An exception a specification admits is named and bounded: requires_pkce: false exempts one confidential client, and is refused for a public one. Secure by default is the rule.

    tenant¶

    Who the provider is, and required. issuer is the identifier every token carries as iss and the URL every endpoint is served under: the routes of the bundle are imported under its path, which the provider checks at the first request. slug names the tenant for good: its identifier is derived from it, and renaming it is creating another tenant. name and look are what the pages show of the tenant, self_served_claims what its people may change of themselves, and authentication_classes the acr it answers with.

    The logo is loaded by the browser of the visitor, under the Content-Security-Policy the application sends. The consent page adds the origin of the logo to its img-src, and no other page does: a logo served from another origin than the pages is named in the img-src of the application's own policy, as the standalone application says of its own.

    The row of the tenant follows the configuration through bin/console beffroi:tenant:synchronize, which is safe to run at every deployment.

    Tenants

    keys¶

    Where the keys of the tenant are sealed and kept, and required. The bundle builds no key management client: kms names one declared under key_management.clients, a DSN or a composite of several, and key_id names the key of that client the keys are sealed under, {tenant} standing for the slug.

    config/packages/beffroi.yaml
    beffroi:
    keys:
        kms: default
        key_id: 'beffroi-{tenant}'
        storage: '%kernel.project_dir%/var/keys'

    storage is the directory of the sealed key files and of the pairwise salt of the tenant, sealed the same way. The salt is drawn on first use and never again, and every pairwise subject of the tenant is computed from it, so the directory is part of what a deployment backs up.

    data_key_secrets lists the secrets every data key of personal data is bound to its row under, which the database does not hold, and is the secret of the kernel when it is left out. The first binds a new key and a key bound under any of them opens, so a secret is replaced by writing the new one first, as personal data says.

    The three other options time the rotation of every key but the SAML keys. A relying party keeps the JWK Set for jwks_max_age, five minutes. A new key is published for staging_delay before it signs, by default jwks_max_age plus the sixty seconds after which Symfony's oidc_login reads the set again for a kid it does not hold. A key works for age, ninety days, before the timed step stages a successor behind it, which is inside the one to three years NIST SP 800-57 Part 1 Rev. 5 §5.3.6 gives a private signature key.

    Key management and keys

    webauthn¶

    Where the FIDO metadata a passkey's attestation is checked against are kept: what beffroi:webauthn:mds-refresh downloads and beffroi:webauthn:mds-import stores. Left out, the directory is var/webauthn of the project. A passkey enrols while it is empty.

    Passkeys

    mutual_tls¶

    For a deployment whose clients authenticate with a certificate (RFC 8705) on a host whose TLS listener asks for one. Named, the discovery document announces every endpoint again under that host, in mtls_endpoint_aliases. Left out, or empty, no alias is announced, which is what a deployment serving one host does.

    config/packages/beffroi.yaml
    beffroi:
    mutual_tls:
        origin: 'https://mtls.op.example.com'

    Mutual TLS

    tokens¶

    How long the codes, requests and tokens the provider issues stand, in seconds. Left out, every lifetime is short: a minute for an authorization code and a pushed request, ten minutes for an access token and an ID token, thirty days of sliding life and ninety in all for a refresh token. The tree bounds them: an authorization code and a request_uri live ten minutes at most (RFC 6749 §4.1.2, RFC 9126 §2.2), a device code thirty minutes, a backchannel request an hour, an interval between two polls thirty seconds, and a refresh token never slides past the absolute lifetime of its family.

    The lifetimes of what a key signs are also how long that key stays published once another key takes over from it, so that a token still in use verifies: ten minutes for a signing key with the defaults. A longer access_token_ttl keeps a retiring key in the JWK Set for longer.

    Tokens

    load¶

    Every request is counted against two budgets, and the section sets their numbers, never whether they exist. The first counts the address of the client, on every route of the bundle and before anything is verified; the second counts a client of a tenant once it has authenticated. A request over a budget is answered 429 with a Retry-After header.

    The address is the one Symfony reads behind the trusted proxies of the application, so a deployment behind a load balancer declares the balancer as a trusted proxy, or every request counts against the address of the balancer. The counts live in the beffroi.load cache pool, on cache.app unless the deployment points the pool at a store its processes share. A number too low on /token or on /jwks refuses the deployment's own clients, so a deployment sets them from what its own clients send.

    journal¶

    How long an act of the journal is kept before the timed step forgets it: 180 days unless the section says otherwise. What a client was given of a person in an act is forgotten with that act. Nothing turns the journal on or off.

    history¶

    How long a change a person made to their claims is kept before the timed step forgets it: 180 days unless the section says otherwise, the default of the journal too, a change and the act that announced it being two halves of one answer.

    geoip¶

    Where the GeoLite2 databases that place the address of a request are kept, and the licence key of the MaxMind account they are downloaded under. MaxMind publishes them under its own licence, so nothing is shipped, and a deployment that names no key downloads nothing: every flow is served, and a provenance says an address and no place.

    config/packages/beffroi.yaml
    beffroi:
    geoip:
        license_key: '%env(GEOIP_LICENSE_KEY)%'

    A weekly timed step downloads them, and bin/console beffroi:geoip:refresh does it at once.

    passwords¶

    The list of compromised passwords a password is compared with before it is written (NIST SP 800-63B §3.1.1.2). How long a password has to be and how hard it is to guess are rules of the core, and no option lowers them. Left out, no list is asked and nothing of a password leaves the deployment.

    config/packages/beffroi.yaml
    beffroi:
    passwords:
        breached:
            endpoint: 'https://api.pwnedpasswords.com/range/'

    Named, the endpoint receives the first five hexadecimal characters of the SHA-1 of a password, and the rest is compared locally. A password the list counts is refused, and so is one the list could not be asked about, within timeout, two seconds. A password already set is looked up again after a login that used it, on the asynchronous lane.

    Passwords

    interaction¶

    How the login_hint of an authorization request is read (OIDC Core §3.1.2.1): sub, the subject the provider issued to that client, by default; username, the name a person signs in with; or none, for a deployment that tags a reading of its own beffroi.oidc.login_hint. It is read one way and never searched, and the login page offers the name of the person it names.

    Interactions

    back_channel_logout¶

    How a logout token is posted to a client that registered a backchannel_logout_uri (Back-Channel Logout 1.0 §2.5): five seconds an attempt, then six retries, two seconds after the first failure and three times longer each time, a minute at most. The retry schedule is the one of every notification the provider carries to a third party, on the async_notifications transport the bundle configures: the logout tokens, the notifications of the backchannel authentication flow, and the pushes that tell a person something waits for them.

    The schedule is also the exp of a logout token, 235 seconds with the defaults, and a retiring signing key stays published that long at least, so that the provider stops trying when what it sends stops being verifiable. An application that routes the notifications to a transport of its own keeps that agreement only if the transport retries with the policy of the bundle; bin/console beffroi:doctor says whether it does.

    Logout and Messenger

    backchannel_authentication¶

    How the login_hint of a backchannel authentication request is read (CIBA Core §7.1), under the three readings of interaction, none being for a deployment that tags its own beffroi.oidc.ciba_user_hint, and how long one call to a client in ping mode may take. Nothing here opens the flow: a client takes part in it by registering its grant and a delivery mode.

    Backchannel authentication

    web_push¶

    Whether the provider reaches people through the push services of their browsers (RFC 8030). Naming a subject turns it on: a mailto: or https: URI at which a push service can reach the operator (RFC 8292 §2.1). Left out, nothing of it is wired, no push leaves the deployment, and the page where a browser would subscribe says so.

    config/packages/beffroi.yaml
    beffroi:
    web_push:
        subject: 'mailto:operator@example.com'

    The push is signed with the application server key of the tenant, drawn like every other key of the tenant:

    bin/console beffroi:keys:generate --algorithm=VAPID

    Web push

    client_authentication¶

    What a client assertion is held to (RFC 7523 §3), how far ahead its exp may be and how much clock it is allowed, and what one read of a jwks_uri may cost: its timeout, its size, how long its keys are kept and how often an unknown kid may read it again. The bounds of a read are those of every published key set the provider reads, the clients' and the trusted issuers'. Which methods a client may authenticate with is the wiring and its registration, and nothing of this section.

    Client authentication

    request_objects¶

    What a request object is held to (RFC 9101 §4), how long it may stand and how much clock it is allowed, and what one read of a request_uri may cost while the browser of a person waits on it. Which algorithm verifies an object is the registration of the client.

    Request objects

    dpop¶

    What a proof of possession is held to (RFC 9449): its age, the clock allowed, and whether it carries a nonce of the provider (RFC 9449 §8). A client that sends no proof is not concerned.

    config/packages/beffroi.yaml
    beffroi:
    dpop:
        nonce:
            required: true

    nonce.required decides which class the container wires, so it is written as true or false in the file.

    DPoP

    trusted_issuers¶

    The issuers whose assertions the tenant accepts as an authorization grant (RFC 7523 §2.1). Whoever holds the signing key of one of them can name a user of the tenant and be answered with an access token, so each one is declared whole: its keys, the algorithms it signs with, the clients that may present its assertions, the source of the identities it names and the scopes it is trusted for. Left out, the grant serves nobody.

    config/packages/beffroi.yaml
    beffroi:
    trusted_issuers:
        'https://workforce.example.com':
            jwks_uri: 'https://workforce.example.com/jwks'
            signing_algorithms: ['ES256']
            clients: ['job-runner']
            identity_provider: workforce
            trusted_scopes: ['invoices.read']

    The sub of an assertion names a user of the tenant only through a linked identity of the source identity_provider names, and no user is ever created from an assertion.

    The JWT bearer grant

    clients¶

    The clients the deployment declares, keyed by client_id, each read like a registration of RFC 7591 §2. A client the configuration does not declare exists only if it registered itself, at the endpoint client_registration opens. A declared client that writes nothing takes the browser flow and its renewal, the scopes openid profile email, and the first wired method of authentication, client_secret_basic, which needs a secret.

    config/packages/beffroi.yaml
    beffroi:
    clients:
        my-application:
            client_name: 'My application'
            secret: '%env(MY_APPLICATION_SECRET)%'
            redirect_uris: ['https://app.example.com/oidc/callback']
            post_logout_redirect_uris: ['https://app.example.com/']
            scope: 'openid profile email offline_access'
        reporting:
            secret: '%env(REPORTING_SECRET)%'
            grant_types: ['client_credentials']
            scope: 'invoices.read'
            resources: ['https://api.example.com/']

    The options of a client hold together or the client is refused, naming the rule:

    • the method of authentication decides what else is registered: a secret for client_secret_basic, client_secret_post and client_secret_jwt, and for no other; keys in jwks or behind jwks_uri for private_key_jwt and self_signed_tls_client_auth; one name of the certificate for tls_client_auth;
    • a client registered for authorization_code registers a redirect URI at least, and a client registered for no grant that sends the browser back registers none;
    • a public client, token_endpoint_auth_method: none, keeps PKCE, writes plain http redirect URIs on 127.0.0.1 or [::1] only, and is refused client_credentials;
    • an encrypted response is named with both its algorithms, and the client publishes a key to encrypt to.

    The secret of a declared client belongs in the environment, as the example reads it, and it is hashed when the client is first read, except for client_secret_jwt, which needs the secret itself.

    Clients and your first client

    client_registration¶

    Whether a client nobody declared may register itself (RFC 7591), and on what authority. Left out, the tenant has no registration endpoint: none in its discovery document, and 404 on the wire. A mode opens it: initial_access_token, a token the tenant issues, limits and revokes; software_statement, a statement signed by an authority the tenant pins under statement_issuers; or open, no credential at all, for a tenant with no real user.

    config/packages/beffroi.yaml
    beffroi:
    client_registration:
        mode: initial_access_token

    bin/console beffroi:client:token:issue 'Partner integration' --registrations=5 --days=7

    The name says later which integration a token was for. Without the options, a token registers one client and is worth thirty days; its value is printed once, and the provider keeps only its digest.

    A client that registered itself is deleted after ninety days without use, or after seven days when it was never used. The rest of the section bounds the read of a sector_identifier_uri and the software statements, names the scopes of a client that asks for none, and, for a tenant whose clients send no code_challenge, exempts its confidential clients from PKCE, never a public one.

    Dynamic registration

    saml¶

    The SAML roles of the tenant, each off unless the section turns it on, and the peers of each. A role that is off wires nothing and answers 404 where its metadata would be, and the peers of a role are refused while it is off.

    Key What it is The page
    identity_provider The tenant as an identity provider, and the rotation of every SAML key The identity provider
    service_providers The applications that sign their users in through it Service providers
    service_provider The tenant as a service provider to identity providers upstream of it Upstream identity providers
    identity_providers The identity providers upstream of it, and the e-mail domains each answers for Upstream identity providers
    config/packages/beffroi.yaml
    beffroi:
    saml:
        identity_provider: true

    Writing a role as a block turns it on as well, unless the block says enabled: false.
